# IM norms library (im-norms)

Feature rules, interaction patterns and page structure of mainstream instant messaging products, split by feature module. Purpose: make ZYMIX PRDs follow IM conventions by default, and explicitly hand the points where conventions are not uniform to Box for a ruling.

## How to use

1. After framing in generation step 0, read only the hit module files per the routing table in SKILL.md section "IM norms"; do not load the whole library.
2. Each module's rules fall into three tiers (defined in the next section): **baseline** is followed by default; **convention** may be written into the card per recommendation and listed in the "Defaulted to recommendation" list; **divergence** is handled by the level mark at the end of the entry: `Level: Must-ask` is asked of Box as a multiple-choice question, `Level: Default-OK` is written into the card per recommendation and listed in the "Defaulted to recommendation" list; divergences that additionally carry `| Framing: yes` at the end are form-type and are asked together during step 0 product framing (levels are defined in "Divergence levels").
3. **Pointer entries**: entries whose body starts with "See XX-nn" (every module has them) mean the concept belongs to another module (see "Cross-module concept ownership table"); they keep only "See owner ID" and a one-sentence scope note, with no options, recommendation, magnitude or level, stay in their original subsection, and when read are handled by the owner entry's tier and level. For entries where only one clause involves an owned concept (such as GRP-13, UX-11, LIST-10, GRP-17), only that clause is changed to "See owner ID" and the other clauses stand as written.
4. **Must-ask states**: dimensions the module requires beyond the general state coverage matrix, each of which must be marked `Covered` / `N/A` / `Undefined`.
5. **Anti-patterns**: after generating, check against them one by one and fix any hit.
6. **Magnitude**: used only to judge whether a ZYMIX value is out of range, **never copy** any competitor's values (ruled 2026-09-15).

## Three-tier definitions and judging discipline

| Tier | Judging threshold | Confidence | How to use at generation | When deviating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | **Every clause** separately meets "direct evidence agrees in ≥ 3 of five products, no mainstream counterexample" | Can only be "High" | Follow by default, do not ask | Write a `Note:` in the card explaining the deviation and list it in the delivery draft's "Decisions for Box"; an unexplained deviation triggers the veto "deviating from baseline" |
| Convention | **Every clause** meets "direct evidence agrees in ≥ 2 products", or "direct evidence from 1 product + explicit text in a platform design guideline, platform review rule or accessibility standard"; one counterexample is allowed, but it cannot rest on inference alone | "Medium" | May follow the entry by default into the card, list it in the delivery draft's "Defaulted to recommendation" list, and mark `suggested` in the working notes (do not write the word "suggested" in the card body) | Does not trigger the "deviating from baseline" veto; after Box overrules, change the card per the ruling |
| Divergence | Mainstream practice differs (two or more practices each with about two products), or only one product's evidence, or purely inference | — | By the level at the end of the entry: `Must-ask` is asked of Box as multiple choice (recommended option first); `Default-OK` is written as card body per the recommended option and listed in "Defaulted to recommendation" | Until a `Must-ask` is ruled, use the `{TBD: topic, recommended: approach}` placeholder (the recommendation is only a direction and is not a conclusion before the ruling); after Box overrules a `Default-OK`, change the card per the ruling |

- **Count products per clause**: when a rule has several clauses (such as "A, and B, and C"), every clause needs its own ≥ 3 products of direct evidence. **Never stitch single-product evidence from different clauses into "three products agree"**; if any clause falls short, the whole entry drops to convention, or that clause is split out into its own entry.
- "Direct evidence" = client strings, open-source code, official help center or developer documentation text. Search snippets, media paraphrase and common-sense inference are not direct evidence.
- "Five products" means the five mainstream products used when re-judging in the evidence files; protocol specs and open-source SDKs are corroboration only and are not counted.
- Conventions likewise **count products per clause**: if any clause has only one product's evidence and no platform spec (including platform design guidelines and platform review rules such as app marketplace review guidelines and developer policies) or accessibility standard text, or only inference, move that clause into must define (it may be written as "for reference"), or turn the whole entry into a divergence. Where a platform spec is the basis, state it in the confidence parenthetical (such as "Medium (direct evidence from one product plus platform spec)"). In one sentence: baseline needs ≥ 3 products per clause, convention needs ≥ 2 products or 1 product + a spec per clause, everything else is a divergence.
- Baselines can only be demoted, never promoted automatically: if the remaining body qualifies for baseline after splitting clauses, the evidence file must state per-clause product counts and the entry moves only after review.
- When a recommendation rationale uses product-count wording such as "most", "three products" or "five products", it must match the product count on that evidence row; when the recommendation goes against the evidence (e.g. all known products pick A but B is recommended), the rationale must say "opposite to what existing products do, a trade-off of ××".

## Divergence levels

Every divergence entry ends with `| Level: Must-ask` or `| Level: Default-OK`, pre-marked by the norms library maintainer using the five categories in the table below. **Must-ask follows the norms library label**: divergences marked `Level: Must-ask`, and clauses an entry says to "treat as must-ask"; those marked `Default-OK` are executed per recommendation as labeled and are not re-judged by the five categories at generation. At run time there are only two extra cases that escalate to must-ask: ④ conflicts with an existing ZYMIX rule or deviates from baseline; a new topic the norms library does not cover that falls within the five categories.

| Level | Criteria (the five categories are for maintaining the norms library and judging new topics outside it; inside the library only ①②③⑤ are pre-marked, ④ see below) | At generation |
|---|---|---|
| Must-ask | ① Privacy defaults, reciprocity rules, what the other party can perceive; ② scope of irreversible handling or data deletion; ③ a binary choice that sends the main flow in different directions and the card cannot be written without settling it; ⑤ reversibility of regulated fund flows: whether a payment can be undone after it is made and whether the funds can be recovered (the refund path and timing depend on licensed institution capabilities, which is a source-of-truth gap in the next row; "whether to refund, and to whom" does not automatically fall under ⑤ by itself, and when privacy or irreversible handling is involved it is judged under ①②) | When the cards of this requirement will write about it, compile it into a multiple-choice question for Box; write `{TBD: topic, recommended: approach}` in the card |
| Default-OK | Everything else (values, copy, minor branches, who can operate, whether to leave a trace, whether to offer a minor feature, etc.) | Write the recommended option as body text, one line in the delivery draft's "Defaulted to recommendation" |
| Source-of-truth gap (do not ask Box) | Values or feasibility that depend on licensed institutions, regulators or engineering capability (limit values, launch region lists, whether in-flight funds can be frozen, refund path and timing, age-judging basis, per-launch-region app marketplace payment rules) | The entry states "this is a source-of-truth gap, write {TBC} into Open items"; write `{TBC: what is missing}` in the card, taking no question quota; Open items are merged by answering party (e.g. "licensed institution: limits, fees, settlement timing" counts as one group), at most 5 groups; `{TBD}` must be cleared before the write gate; if an external answer has not arrived, `{TBC}` may be listed one by one in the write request block (answering party, which card is affected) and, with Box's agreement, go onto the canvas as is and be filled in when the answer arrives; the part of the same entry that needs a Box trade-off is handled by level as usual |

- **Form-type divergences**: the entry carries `| Framing: yes` after the level (such as real-time location form, whether split bills go through bookkeeping or real payment, group type system, channel comment section, default encryption scope, in-group AI invocation form), deciding "what form to build". These are asked together with the chat form in one round of multiple-choice during generation step 0 product framing (one dimension per question, ≤ 4 questions) and **do not count toward the question budget's 4 questions**; more than 4 framing dimensions means the requirement is too large, so first ask Box whether to split; after framing, the sample and cards write "determined as ×× at framing" and no longer leave a `{TBD}`.
- Category ④ of the question budget, "conflicts with an existing ZYMIX rule or deviates from baseline", is only known at run time and is not pre-marked in the library: if an entry marked `Default-OK`, a convention or a baseline conflicts with the ZYMIX source of truth, it is escalated to must-ask at run time. Apart from ④ and new topics outside the library, run time does not escalate an entry because it "looks like it involves" a category.
- Individual parameters in a baseline or convention's "must define" may also fall under ①② (such as whether content the other party already downloaded is kept after an unsend); such parameters are marked "treat as must-ask" within the entry, and the other must-define parameters are taken per recommendation or common sense and listed in "Defaulted to recommendation".
- For entries whose recommendation reads "per existing ZYMIX rules", check the source of truth at run time first; once found, write per the source of truth and do not ask.

## Entry format

```
- **[ID] Rule name**: practice. Must define: parameters or branches the PRD must spell out. | Magnitude: range (for judging magnitude only) | Confidence: High/Medium
- **[ID] Topic name**: Option A: … / Option B: …. Recommended: X. Rationale: …. | Level: Must-ask/Default-OK
- **[ID] Topic name**: Option A: … / Option B: …. Recommended: X. Rationale: …. | Level: Must-ask | Framing: yes
```

- IDs look like `SEND-03` and are referenced only inside the skill, reviews and evals, **never written onto the canvas** (rule IDs inside PRDs were retired 2026-08-26). Once assigned, an ID is not renamed; when an entry changes tier (baseline ↔ convention ↔ divergence) the ID stays and only its position moves; when retired it is marked `(retired)` and the slot is kept.
- Confidence: High = meets the baseline threshold; Medium = meets the convention threshold; Low = inference or a single product, which may appear only in a divergence's trade-off background.
- A divergence entry must end with `| Level: Must-ask` or `| Level: Default-OK` (checked by `lint_skill.py`).
- Magnitude records only product constraints that can be written into annotation cards, such as counts, numbers of items, numbers of people, duration tiers, or a qualitative description; **visual and motion values such as pixels, logical pixels and milliseconds go only in evidence**, never in module files.
- Module files **contain no brand names** (including product line names).
- Category words such as "mainstream products", "most products", "some products", "mainstream domestic products", "privacy-first products" and "one product" are **for the skill's internal trade-off judgment only** and must not be written into annotation card body or `Note:`; what the card writes is the rule itself. Exception: "consistent with mainstream instant messaging products" is a rewording Box approved (see SKILL.md annotation card writing norms), is not on the banned list, and may be added in a `Note:` as one sentence, but cannot replace the rule itself. Module wording also must not be copied into a card whole sentences at a time; rewrite per the annotation card writing norms.

## Calibration sample format

The calibration sample at the end of each module is the agent's strongest imitation target, so all follow these rules:

- Only `Must-ask` level values are written `{TBD: topic, recommended: approach}` (such as `{TBD: whether forwarded copies are also unsent, recommended: keep the copies}`); values that depend on licensed institutions, regulators or engineering capability are written `{TBC: what is missing}`, and multiple `{TBC}` for the same answering party are merged into one statement in the opening and the `Note:`; where the recommendation reads "per existing ZYMIX rules" and the source of truth already has the answer, write it as body text per the source of truth; for `Framing: yes` forms, open the sample with "determined as ×× at framing"; conventions, `Default-OK` divergences and ordinary parameters inside must define are written directly as the recommended or illustrative value (within the magnitude), and the opening sentence of the sample says which are written per recommendation and which are must-ask. Runtime variables are written as usual, `{nickname}` `{N}`.
- A check must be a **precondition check** after the tap and before the result appears; branches of each check are exhaustive in pairs with mutually different results, and the last branch states the destination; contradictions such as "a toggle that is not displayed being tapped" must not appear.
- With fewer than 3 checks, do not start a decision chain; write plain steps and do not pad out checks.
- Check order: filters such as cooldown, rate limit, lock and blocklist go first; checks that do not depend on the server (permission, lock, network) go before checks that depend on the server's return (server state, balance, eligibility); when the filter itself depends on the server (such as whether an in-flight request already exists, or a redemption count limit), it goes after the network check and before the other server checks.
- Unified wording: rule-type times are written "by the account's time zone" (basis in UX-36); the privacy page is written "Settings" - "Privacy & Data" (see PRIV-21); the system notification vehicle is written "platform system chat" (see BOT-09).
- Hard format of decision chains: checks use repeated `a.`, exits use repeated `i.`; the `1-check name` prefix sits tight against `a.`; each exit takes its own line; when there are must-ask items a `Note:` listing the items awaiting ruling (unnumbered) always ends the card, and when there are none it is not written.
- Button cards fix two opening lines: `1. The button lights up after <input condition>`, `2. On tap, check in order:`; when the entry needs stating, put `Entry:` at the start of line 1, in the same line as the lit-up condition, and put the other rules after the decision chain.
- Wording matches `anti-patterns.md`: "delete for me only", "unsend for everyone"; multiple parallel values use `/`, not `|`; buttons or options in parallel are written "A"/"B", each value quoted separately, not "A / B".
- The sample must pass `scripts/lint_card_text.py` with 0 hits.

## External research material (not included in the public version)

Module files carry only condensed rules; each product's specific values, evidence source text and evidence conflict notes live in research material outside the norms library (the author's local Wiki, not in the public version). When you need to judge a value's magnitude or tell the decision-maker how each product does it, consult your own research material; content with brand names must be rewritten per the brand-name ban before going into a delivery draft.

## Collection and freshness

- Collected 2026-10-30; re-judged into three tiers 2026-10-30. Sources: public behavior of mainstream clients, open-source client code, official help centers and developer documentation, protocol specs. Raw records are not published with the public version.
- Most values are client defaults and the server may override them; values involving paid tiers are marked separately.
- Re-review every 90 days or when Box asks; for volatile platform facts (system permissions, push policy) check the official documentation when online, never from memory.
