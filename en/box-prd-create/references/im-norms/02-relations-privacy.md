## Baseline (follow by default, high confidence)
- **[REL-01] Add by account identifier search**: the other party can be searched and added by an account identifier (phone number, username or account ID, etc.). Must define: which identifiers are supported; whether each identifier can be found and the corresponding privacy switch; whether "no result" and "restricted by the other party's privacy" are distinguished and their copy. | Confidence: High
- **[REL-07] After blocking, messages and calls do not reach**: the blocked party cannot deliver messages or start calls. Must define: what the blocked party sees after sending, see REL-22; what happens to the existing chat and chat history after blocking, messages in flight before the block takes effect, and whether messages sent by the other party during the block are delivered after unblocking (these three belong to data-deletion scope; treat as must-ask). | Confidence: High
- **[REL-08] Blocking also cuts off profile-type visible content**: after blocking, the blocked party cannot see your profile-type visible content (such as online status, avatar, feed posts). Must define: list item by item the visible items cut off (how avatar, online, signature, feed posts and so on are presented to the blocked party is about what the other party can perceive; treat the whole as must-ask and merge into one question); when the question stem or rationale says "indistinguishable from the other party being offline or having online status turned off", also point out the exception: cutting off the avatar itself can be noticed (the blocked party still sees your real avatar in shared groups), for Box to decide knowingly; whether your "last seen" is invisible to the blocked party or degrades to the vaguest tier (this entry is the only place that defines this question; the online-status entry in module 04 only references this one; it is about what the other party can perceive, treat as must-ask). | Confidence: High
- **[REL-09] The block list can be undone**: settings have a centralized block-list management entry (list, empty state, unblock one by one); the block feature must ship with it in the same release, not under "suggested follow-ups"; blocking can be lifted at any time, restoring two-way reachability. Unblocking also goes through a second confirmation (unblocking restores the other party's reach, so it cannot take effect on one tap). Must define: confirmation copy for unblocking, whether history is kept after unblocking, and whether blocking and unblocking sync across all of the person's logged-in devices. | Confidence: High
- **[REL-11] Group invitations are limited by the invitee's privacy**: the invitee can restrict being pulled directly into groups through a "who can invite me to groups" setting. Must define: the failure-reason copy the inviter sees, the alternative path (such as sending an invite link or adding as a contact first), and the summary copy when some of a batch invitation fails. Direct pulling restricted by the invitee's privacy is defined only in this entry; the invitation entries of the group module must not offer an option that bypasses this entry. | Confidence: High
- **[REL-12] Contact with strangers must have a gate**: there must be a control on contact with strangers, form see REL-21, recipient-side interception see REL-31; a sender blocked by the gate sees a clear reason rather than no response. | Confidence: High
- **[REL-14] Privacy items are set independently and sync across devices**: each visibility setting is independent, and after a change takes effect immediately on all logged-in devices. Must define: the default tier of each item, and the retroactivity rule for existing data (historical feed posts, profile info already sent) after a change. | Confidence: High

## Conventions (default-OK, mark as suggested)

- **[REL-02] Username uniqueness and character set**: usernames are globally unique and case-insensitive, and can be deleted and released; the character set is a limited set (one product allows only lowercase letters, digits and underscore, another also allows hyphen), with the specifics set by ZYMIX. Must define: the allowed character set, first-character restriction (can it start with a digit), minimum and maximum length, four kinds of inline prompts ("already taken", "contains illegal characters", "too short", "too long"), and change frequency. | Magnitude: minimum 3 to 5 characters, maximum a few dozen characters | Confidence: Medium (only two products)
- **[REL-03] Contact remarks are visible only to oneself**: a contact remark takes effect only in the person's own view and is invisible to the other party. Must define: remark length limit, whether tags are supported, display priority among the remark, the address-book name and the other party's profile name, and whether it syncs to other devices. | Magnitude: remark limit on the order of a hundred characters | Confidence: Medium (visible only to oneself has direct evidence from only two products; display priority and tags each have evidence from only one product and have been moved to must-define)
- **[REL-04] The "contact joined" prompt can be turned off**: after an address-book contact registers, a prompt can be shown, with its own switch. Must define: the default switch state and the number of prompts per person. | Confidence: Medium (only two products)
- **[REL-05] Limit high-disturbance actions until the other party accepts**: before the other party accepts or replies, the sender's high-disturbance actions (such as consecutive sends, proactive calls) are restricted, and a reason prompt is shown when restricted. Must define: the number of messages that can be sent before a reply, whether calling is allowed, the number of strangers that can be proactively contacted per day, and the over-limit copy; for the recipient-side interception switch see also REL-31. | Magnitude: 1 to single digits of messages before a reply; the daily cap on proactively contacted people was not obtained, set by the PRD | Confidence: Medium (three products each limit a different kind of action in a different form, and the applicable scenario of one is unconfirmed)
- **[REL-06] If friend verification requests are adopted, they need their own entry and states**: requests must have their own list or entry, not mixed into ordinary chats, and each request's handling state is visible in the list. Must define: the state set (e.g. pending, accepted, declined, expired, filtered), request validity period, re-request rule after expiry, and the entry for filtered requests. | Magnitude: evidence did not obtain the validity in days, set by the PRD | Confidence: Medium (conditional rule, only two products; expiry and filtered each have evidence from only one product and have been moved to must-define)
- **[REL-10] After removing a contact, later messages from them return to the stranger path**: applies only to products adopting a request or verification model; after removal, messages the other party sends are handled by stranger rules (re-verification needed or becoming a message request) and no longer enjoy the contact channel; under an address-book model this path does not exist. Must define: what happens to the chat and history before and after removal (see REL-23). | Confidence: Medium (conditional rule, only two products)
- **[REL-24] Privacy tiers include per-person exceptions**: besides tiers, visibility settings support exceptions for specific contacts. Must define: the tiers each item supports (e.g. everyone/contacts/nobody), the exception list cap, whether the settings list shows exceptions as a count summary, and handling when an exception target is deleted or blocked; for the exception list form see REL-30. | Confidence: Medium (exceptions are consistent across three products but differ in form, and one is contact-level permission; three-tier tiers two products, count summary one product, all only in must-define)
- **[REL-26] Feed-type content visibility is independent of blocking**: applies only to products with feed posts or stories; their visibility scope must be settable independently of blocking (exclusion list, specified people, or "don't let them see / don't see them"). Must define: the default visibility scope (see FEED-20), caps on excluded and specified people, whether a "don't see them" direction is offered, and the retroactivity rule for historical posts. | Confidence: Medium (conditional rule, consistent across three products but differing in form)
- **[REL-13] Mutual invisibility is reciprocal**: (reciprocity rules are privacy-type and treated as must-ask: the delivery draft lists them under "Decisions for Box", recommending reciprocity) after turning off one's own "last seen", one also cannot see the other party's last seen, and the setting's description states this reciprocity; for the reciprocity rule of the read receipt switch see SEND-16. Must define: whether typing status and other visible items are equally reciprocal; exceptions to reciprocity (e.g. sending a message makes one visible online to the other party, visibility when never chatted with the other party and not saved as a contact); whether a paid tier may look one-way. | Confidence: Medium (last-seen reciprocity has direct evidence from only two products, one of which lets a paid tier look one-way; typing-status reciprocity has evidence from only one product and has been moved to must-define)

## Divergences (ask Box on must-ask; write default-OK per recommendation and list it)
- **[REL-21] Stranger contact model**: Option A: anyone can message directly, limited only by privacy settings / Option B: a stranger's first message goes into "message requests" first, and becomes a chat only after acceptance, with no read receipt sent before acceptance / Option C: friend verification request, chat only after approval. Recommended: B. Rationale: privacy first and no extra friend concept needed; if ZYMIX already has a friend system, follow existing rules and choose C. Background: A is adopted by two products, B by one, C by two. | Level: Must-ask
- **[REL-22] Feedback to the blocked party after sending**: Option A: looks the same as a normal send, but is never delivered and never shows read (silent) / Option B: a generic failure prompt without the word "block" / Option C: explicitly shows rejection (e.g. "Message sent, but rejected by the other party"). Recommended: A. Rationale: opposite to the explicit-rejection practice of mainstream domestic products (overseas products had no official statement obtained, and the question stem must not say "mainstream is mostly explicit" or "mainstream is mostly silent"); it is a privacy trade-off: the block decision is not revealed to the blocked party, and it is consistent with not disclosing the reason when calls are restricted (see MISC-26). Background: C has direct evidence from two mainstream domestic products; A has only one product's help-text corroboration (being blocked is listed as one reason read receipts are not visible), Medium confidence; B has not been seen adopted by any product. When serving domestic users who require it to be perceptible, choose C. | Level: Must-ask
- **[REL-23] Chat history when removing a contact**: Option A: clear chat history at the same time / Option B: keep the chat, only end the relation / Option C: a dialog lets the user tick "also delete chat history". For interception when unhandled payments (pending collections, unclaimed red packets) exist before removal see PAY-26. Recommended: C. Rationale: data integrity first, and mistaken deletion is avoided. | Level: Must-ask
- **[REL-25] First-release scope of privacy settings**: Option A: only "who can contact me", "who can invite me to groups", "who can find me" / Option B: A plus online status, avatar, profile bio / Option C: B plus calls, forward attribution, voice messages, birthday. For the "allow adding me via contact card" switch see RICH-44. Recommended: B. Rationale: covers core visibility with controllable scope. | Level: Default-OK
- **[REL-27] Username form**: Option A: optional public username, no suffix / Option B: username plus a system-assigned numeric suffix, search requires the full string / Option C: system account ID only, no custom username. Recommended: A. Rationale: smallest change, and searchability is backstopped by the REL-01 switch. | Level: Default-OK
- **[REL-28] Semantics of blocking a group**: Option A: blocking a group = leave the group and refuse later messages, and members cannot pull you in again / Option B: mute only, do not leave / Option C: no group blocking, only leave and block the inviter or admin. Recommended: A. Rationale: the anti-harassment loop is complete. | Level: Must-ask
- **[REL-29] Read receipt and typing status switches**: See SEND-22 (switch form); for the switch reciprocity rule see SEND-16; for whether to offer one-on-one read receipts see SEND-09.
- **[REL-30] Form of the privacy exception list**: Option A: "exclude" list only / Option B: two lists, "always allow" and "always deny" / Option C: contact-level one-way block (don't let them see, don't see them). Three products each use one form. Recommended: B. Rationale: privacy first, it can both tighten and loosen, and can show a summary like "Contacts (-3, +2)". | Level: Default-OK
- **[REL-31] Interception side for strangers' high-frequency contact**: Option A: sender-side limits only (limit message count before a reply, daily proactive-contact cap) / Option B: plus an optional recipient switch that intercepts high-frequency messages from unknown accounts and can mute unknown calls / Option C: no rate limiting, rely on blocking and reporting. Recommended: B. Rationale: the recipient is in control and contacts are unaffected (privacy first); sender-side limit values are set by business (magnitude see REL-05). | Level: Default-OK

## Must-ask states (module-specific; supplements the general state matrix)
- Other party's state: has blocked me, deleted account, has removed me; when I am in the same group as the other party, the visibility of in-group posts and @.
- Blocking (the following are required companions of the block feature, written in this release, not under suggested follow-ups): centralized block-list management entry (see REL-09); two-way interception of group invitations (see REL-11, GRP-17); how the blocked party's messages appear on the blocker's side when others quote or reply to them in a group; messages the other party is sending at the moment the block takes effect; whether messages sent by the other party during the block are delivered after unblocking; display in groups both belong to.
- Multi-device: another device has blocked or unblocked; another device has accepted or declined the same request.
- Special objects: the other party is an official or system account (whether blocking is possible, see BOT-28); mutual blocking; the basis for the moment a block takes effect (the server receive time is the cutoff).
- Request chain: request expired, the other party deleted their account, duplicate request, the other party changed privacy settings at the time of the request.
- Stranger messages: a stranger's first message received while offline, the same person writing again after the request was deleted, the regret entry after a request is accepted (block or report).
- Privacy settings: account logged out mid-change; an object the setting depends on (a contact) is deleted; online exposure from sending a message while status is hidden; exception items such as group receipts after turning receipts off; display on linked devices when settings can be changed only on the primary device.
- Interception switch: what the intercepted unknown-account sender sees while it is on; whether intercepted messages are delivered once the rate recovers; the missed-call prompt after a stranger's call is muted.
- Group invitation: invitee's privacy rejection, invitee has blocked the inviter, invite link expired.
- Removing a contact: risk prompt when there are shared unfinished items within 24 hours; for interception of unhandled transfers and red packets see PAY-26.

## Anti-patterns
| Vague/wrong | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "The other party cannot send messages after blocking" | State what prompt the blocked party sees, whether the message stays on the sender's side, and whether it is delivered after unblocking | Stating only the effect and not the sender's feedback leaves engineering to decide |
| "Strangers can message, verification required" | First determine the model (direct chat / request / application), then state the limits before acceptance | The three models have entirely different state machines |
| "Privacy setting: everyone / friends / nobody" | List the setting items one by one and state each item's tiers, exception list and reciprocity | The tiers differ between items |
| "Clear records after removing a contact" | State whether it is ticked, whether the other party is notified, and the path of their later messages | Removal semantics diverge in three places |
| "Username is unique" | State character set, first character, length, four prompts, change frequency | Missing any one makes it impossible to accept |
| "Show failure when restricted" | State the restriction reason, whether there is an alternative path, and retry timing | Without a reason prompt, users retry repeatedly |

## Calibration sample
The feature most easily written vaguely: blocking. After a tap there is only one pre-check, "is there a network", so no decision chain. What the blocked party sees when sending or calling, and how last seen appears to them, are about what the other party can perceive, must-ask, with placeholders left on the card; record retention, multi-device sync and whether messages are delivered after unblocking are written as body text per recommendation.

Description:
1. The "Block" button on the other party's profile page is always lit; on tap a dialog: "After blocking, you will no longer receive their messages and calls.", with buttons "Cancel" and "Block"
2. On tapping "Block" in the dialog, when offline: toast: "Network unavailable. Check your connection and try again", and the state stays unblocked
3. When online: blocking takes effect, toast: "Blocked", the profile button becomes "Unblock"; the blocked state syncs to all devices you are logged in on
4. After blocking takes effect:
   a. The other party sends a message: the message is not delivered, and the other party sees {TBD: feedback to the blocked party after sending, recommended: same as a normal send but never delivered}
   a. The other party starts a call: not connected, and the other party sees {TBD: feedback to the blocked party on calling, recommended: show no answer}
   a. The other party views your profile: cannot see your avatar and online status, and your last seen appears to them as {TBD: last seen as seen by the blocked party, recommended: invisible}
   a. You send a message in the chat input bar: dialog: "You have blocked this person. Unblock to send messages.", with buttons "Cancel" and "Unblock"
5. Your chat and chat history with the other party stay in your list and are not deleted by blocking
6. Messages the other party sends during the block are not delivered; after unblocking, {TBD: whether messages sent during the block are delivered, recommended: not delivered}
