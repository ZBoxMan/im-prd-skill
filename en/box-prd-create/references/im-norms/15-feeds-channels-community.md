# Feed posts, stories, channels, communities, and topics

> Applies: read this file when the PRD involves feed posts (image-and-text content feed), stories (time-limited posts), channels (one-way broadcast), communities (multi-group aggregation and announcement groups), in-group topics, and reply threads. Whether to introduce channels, communities, and topics as forms at all is covered in GRP-15. Evidence is in evidence/15-feeds-channels-community.md.

## Baseline (follow by default, high confidence)
- **[FEED-01] Audience is selectable per post**: when publishing a feed post or story, the audience can be chosen, with at least two list-based tiers: "everyone except some people" and "only some people". Must define: the tier list and copy, the default tier (see FEED-20), the blocking copy when a list tier is chosen but the list is empty, list sources (contacts, groups, import from a group) and the headcount cap, whether an "only me" tier is offered; lists and block are independent of each other, see REL-26. | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: High
- **[FEED-02] Stories have a fixed lifecycle**: after publishing, story content automatically becomes invisible to viewers on expiry; the default duration is 24 hours. Must define: the timer start (based on the server's publish-success time), how a viewer who is watching at the moment of expiry is handled, whether the duration is selectable (see FEED-24), where the story goes on the author's side after expiry (see FEED-23), and the placeholder copy for an expired story quoted in a chat. | Magnitude: default 24 hours; selectable tiers 6 to 48 hours | Confidence: High
- **[FEED-03] Give an explicit state when content is not visible**: when a feed post or story that is opened or interacted with has been deleted, has expired, or is not visible to me, show explicit state copy; do not show a blank screen or a generic error. Must define: whether the deleted, expired, and not-visible copy are distinguished (distinguishing them lets the other party learn they were removed from the audience, which is what the other party can perceive, so treat as must-ask), and the placeholder copy when the client version is too old; for in-flight interactions that hit vanished original content, see FEED-08. | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: High
- **[FEED-04] Publishing has hard limits**: publishing feed posts and stories has hard limits (at least one of: media count, video duration, caption length). Must define: image count cap, video duration lower and upper bounds, whether images and videos can be mixed in one post, caption character cap, the blocking copy for empty content, how over-limit is handled (block immediately with a prompt at selection time, or auto-trim and split into segments with a prompt), and the prompt for unsupported types shared in from other apps. | Magnitude: images single digits to a dozen or so; story video tens of seconds to a minute and a half; caption several hundred to two thousand characters | Confidence: High

## Conventions (default-OK, mark as suggested)

- **[FEED-05] Replying to a story goes to a one-on-one chat with the author**: a viewer's text replies and reactions to a story land in the one-on-one chat with the author, shown as quoting that story, and do not become public comments. Must define: how the quote block appears in the chat (including the "Story" tag), the placeholder for the quote block after the story expires or is deleted, whether the author can turn off replies and reactions as a whole and the default, and where replies to in-group stories go. | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products; the other product is a search summary)
- **[FEED-06] The viewer list is visible to the author only**: a story's viewer list is open only to the author; viewers cannot see each other. Must define: the copy when nobody has viewed, how long the list is retained (expires with the story or is set separately), whether the author can directly "hide it from them" or block someone from within the list, and whether it is affected by the receipt switch (see FEED-22). | Magnitude: the list is retained until about one day after the story expires | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products; the other product only shows view records linked to the receipt switch)
- **[FEED-07] Deleting your own feed post needs a second confirmation**: the author confirms a second time before deleting their own feed post, and a failed deletion gives a retryable prompt. Must define: the confirmation copy and the failure copy; whether others' comments and likes under that post are deleted with it, and how related entries in others' interaction messages are displayed (deleting others' interactions together falls under the data deletion scope, treat as must-ask; cascading deletion of comments is stated by only one product, move it into must-define). | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-08] Failed publishing and interactions can be retried**: when sending a feed post, story, comment, or like fails, show an unsent state in place and offer retry; do not silently discard. Must define: placeholder copy for each kind of failure, the retry and delete entry points, the copy when a multi-audience send partially succeeds, whether failed content is visible only on the publishing device; whether an in-flight comment, like, or reply that hits original content that was deleted, expired, or became not visible to me prompts "not sent" and the reason (for reference: one product prompts "This content was deleted, comment not sent"). | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-09] Authors can pin their own feed posts**: the author can pin and unpin their own feed posts on their personal feed page. Must define: the pin cap and handling when full, the failure copy for pinning and unpinning, and how a pinned post is handled after it is deleted or expires (the pin cap value comes from only one product, move it into must-define). | Magnitude: single digits | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-10] Channels publish under the channel identity**: only admins publish in a channel, content is signed with the channel name and avatar rather than an individual, and subscribers cannot speak in the channel. Must define: whether the publishing admin's signature can optionally be attached and the default, what subscribers can do (react, vote; for comments see FEED-31), and admin appointment and dismissal and owner exit (same as groups, see GRP-03 and GRP-28). | Magnitude: subscriber count can reach hundreds of thousands or more, or be uncapped | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-11] State the consequence when leaving a channel**: confirm a second time before leaving a channel, stating that updates from the channel will no longer be received. Must define: the confirmation copy, whether the chat is removed from the list after leaving, and the path to rejoin after leaving (a private channel requires getting the link again). | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-12] A channel is either discoverable or joinable by link only**: a channel can be set to be searchable or discoverable in a directory, or to be joinable only via an invite link, switched by the owner or an admin. Must define: the default tier, how existing links and existing subscribers are handled when switching between the two states, whether joining after approval or after answering a question is supported (approval rules see GRP-08), link invalidation rules (same as groups, see GRP-07), and the cap on public channels each account can create. | Magnitude: each account about ten public channels | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products; the other product only shows the join-method setting)
- **[FEED-13] Channel invalid and dissolved states**: when a channel is banned, dissolved, or inaccessible to me, opening it gives an explicit state and next step; do not show a blank screen. Must define: the copy for banned, dissolved, and inaccessible; whether a subscriber's chat is kept read-only or removed; the second-confirmation copy for dissolving (stating it cannot be restored) and whether subscribers are notified. | Magnitude: not applicable | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-14] Communities have their own admin layer**: a community (an aggregation of multiple groups) has community-level admins who can add and remove groups within the community and remove members. Must define: the community admin cap, the relationship between community admins and sub-group admins, whether removal from the community also removes the person from all of its sub-groups, whether a group can belong to multiple communities at once, whether a group removed from a community stays as a standalone group or is dissolved, and the caps on groups and total members in a community with the full-community prompt. | Magnitude: community admins about twenty; a community can contain about a hundred groups, with total members in the thousands | Confidence: Medium (direct evidence from two products)
- **[FEED-15] Not viewing someone's feed posts and stories**: See REL-26. Applies: not viewing someone's feed posts, hiding someone's story, and muting someone's status are all handled in the "don't view them" direction of that entry.
- **[FEED-16] Feed posts and stories after block**: See REL-08. Applies: the visibility of feed posts and stories to the blocked party after block.
- **[FEED-17] Account-level "hide from them" list**: See REL-30. Applies: an exclusion list that is not per post and takes effect on all feed posts and stories; for tiers and the exception cap see REL-24.
- **[FEED-18] Community announcements are published by admins only**: See GRP-02. Applies: the announcement group in a community where only admins can publish.
- **[FEED-19] Encryption state of channels and stories**: See SAFE-01. Applies: whether channels and stories are end-to-end encrypted, and the protection state after forwarding to a one-on-one chat.

## Divergences (ask Box on must-ask; write default-OK per recommendation and list it)
- **[FEED-20] Default audience for feed posts and stories**: Option A: default visible only to contacts, the publish page explicitly shows the current audience, and it can be changed to everyone per post / Option B: default to the last selection / Option C: default visible to everyone (including strangers and followers). Whether minor accounts are locked to no wider than contacts, and how many posts a non-contact can see on a personal profile page, belong to the same topic; for the age determination basis and which tier applies to accounts that have not completed age confirmation, see AGE-07. Recommended: A, and do not offer the "everyone" tier to accounts determined to be minors. Rationale: privacy and data integrity first; three known products default to contacts only (one of them does not offer an "everyone" tier for feed posts); if production defaults to visible to everyone, that conflicts with age-assurance compliance risk. Consequence note: if AGE-07 rules that accounts without completed age confirmation are treated as minors, and production new accounts have no birthday filled in, almost all new accounts cannot choose "everyone", and an age-confirmation entry must be provided at the same time; the question stem must state this. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-21] Who can see feed post interactions (likes, comments)**: Option A: visible only to the author and people who are "also the author's contacts", and interactions from non-mutual contacts are not visible to each other / Option B: visible to everyone who can see the post / Option C: visible to the author only. Recommended: A. Rationale: privacy: a commenter may not want to be seen by the author's other contacts, and interactions should not expose the author's contact relationships; Background: A appears in only one product and with low confidence; interactions on stories go to a one-on-one chat per FEED-05 and are naturally visible to the author only. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-22] Story view records and the receipt switch**: Option A: view records follow the global read receipt switch, and someone who turns it off neither leaves a view record nor sees others' (for the reciprocity rule see SEND-16) / Option B: stories get their own "view receipt" switch, also reciprocal / Option C: view records are always left, with a separate time-limited incognito viewing with a cooldown (paid tier). Recommended: B. Rationale: privacy: watching a story and reading a one-on-one chat are two different scenarios, a separate switch lets the user turn off just one, and reciprocity prevents watching without being seen. Background: A, B, and C are each from one product. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-23] Retention after a story expires**: Option A: deleted from the server on expiry, and not kept on the author's side either / Option B: moved after expiry into an archive visible only to the author, which can be deleted manually / Option C: B plus "publish to personal profile" for long-term display, with an audience set separately per item. Recommended: B. Rationale: data integrity: the author does not lose their own content, while viewers still see it as expired; C turns time-limited content into long-term display, contrary to what a story is expected to be. Background: A is two products (one of which also has an internal experimental archive), C is one product; B is a compromise not seen standalone in existing products, a data-integrity tradeoff. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-24] Whether story duration is selectable**: Option A: fixed at 24 hours / Option B: selectable tiers at publish time (such as 6, 12, 24, 48 hours). Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: viewers have a stable expectation that "a story disappears after a day"; two products fix it at 24 hours, and the other fixes it on the free tier with only the paid tier selectable. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-25] Editing the audience of a single post after publishing**: Option A: not editable, can only delete and repost / Option B: editable at any time and takes effect immediately, and the edit page states that people who have already seen it cannot be taken back. Whether an account-level list change applies retroactively to already published content, see REL-26. Recommended: B. Rationale: recoverability: when the wrong audience was chosen, the user does not have to delete and repost and lose existing interactions. Background: one product can edit at any time, one can only delete and repost, and one is in a gradual rollout, not confirmed. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-26] Historical visibility window for feed posts**: Option A: not offered / Option B: an account-level setting "contacts can only see my posts from a recent period", with tiers such as three days, one month, six months, all. Recommended: A. Rationale: the first release already has two controls (per-post audience and deletion), and how a time window applies to old content is hard for users to understand; only one product offers it, and its effect on old content is contradictory across sources. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-27] Mentioning others in a story**: Option A: not offered / Option B: mentions allowed (single-digit cap), the mentioned person receives a one-on-one chat prompt, and a mentioned person outside the audience can also see that story / Option C: same as B, but only people within the audience can be mentioned. Recommended: C. Rationale: privacy: the audience set by the publisher is not bypassed by the mention action. Background: one product is B (cap of 5), and one only leaves a "mentioned you in a story" system message in the chat. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-28] Scope of feed interaction reminders**: Option A: remind only for interactions directly related to me (comments or likes on my post, replies to my comment, mentions of me) / Option B: beyond A, also remind for others' later interactions under posts I have interacted with, which can be turned off separately. For push switch layering and mute silencing see NOTI-01 and NOTI-04. Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: reminders not directly related easily become interruptions; one product offers B and it can be turned off. The reminder form must be stated: recommend an in-app red dot with a count, with no per-item system push (if push is wanted, send it merged and give it a separate switch). | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-29] Whether to introduce channels, communities, and topics**: See GRP-15. Applies: the channel, community, and topic entries in this module are used only after GRP-15 rules that the corresponding form is introduced.
- **[FEED-30] Visibility of subscriber and community member lists**: Option A: the channel subscriber list and the community announcement group member list are visible to admins only, members see only the total count, and subscribing and joining do not expose contact details such as phone numbers / Option B: all members can view the list. Recommended: A. Rationale: privacy: subscribing to a channel or joining a community should not expose oneself to strangers; Background: one product is A in both channels and communities, and one lets people view member information after joining, which is B. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-31] Channel comment section**: Option A: no comments, subscribers can only react with emoji and vote / Option B: each post has a comment section (a reply thread rooted at the post), admins can turn it off per channel or per post, and joining the discussion group first can be required before commenting / Option C: the channel is split into sub-channels, with posts and discussion carried in separate sections. Recommended: A. Rationale: fewest states in the first release: a comment section needs accompanying moderation, banning commenters, and comment notifications; introduce B when there is a clear interaction need. Background: A, B, and C are each from one product. | Level: Must-ask | Framing: yes
- **[FEED-32] Channel content retention and what new subscribers can see**: Option A: kept permanently, new subscribers can see all history, and admins can enable automatic deletion by duration / Option B: a fixed server-side retention period (about 30 days), and new subscribers can see only updates within the retention period / Option C: kept permanently with no automatic deletion offered. Recommended: A. Rationale: data integrity with control: subscribers see the full channel, and admins keep deletion control. Background: one product is A, one is B. | Level: Must-ask
- **[FEED-33] Subscribers contacting the channel**: Option A: not offered, subscribers can only react / Option B: subscribers can send direct messages to the channel, admins reply under the channel identity without exposing personal accounts, and paid or free pass-through can be set / Option C: B plus subscriber submissions, which admins review, edit, schedule, or reject. Recommended: A. Rationale: smallest scope for the first release; direct messages and submissions need extra moderation and anti-harassment capability. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-34] How community members join sub-groups**: Option A: community members can join sub-groups visible to them directly without an invite link, and admins can set a sub-group to hidden (visible only to admins and that group's members) / Option B: joining a sub-group requires an invite or admin approval. For group join approval rules see GRP-08. Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: the value of a community lies in members being able to discover and enter related groups, and the hidden tier keeps private groups. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-35] Who can add groups to a community**: Option A: by default all members can add, and admins can tighten it to admins only / Option B: by default only admins can add, and members can submit suggestions for admin approval. Recommended: B. Rationale: opposite to existing products' practice, a controllability tradeoff: a member adding a group exposes that group to the whole community, so tightening by default prevents abuse, and members can still submit suggestions. Background: two products default to A (one of which is third-party retelling), and both allow admins to tighten. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-36] How a community appears in the chat list**: Option A: collapsed into one expandable entry, and members can switch to showing each group individually / Option B: a separate community tab or entry / Option C: shown only per group, one by one. Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: a dozen groups in one community do not crowd the chat list, and expanding reaches them directly. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-37] Semantics of leaving a community**: Option A: leaving the community equals leaving the announcement group, with an optional "also leave the groups in the community" checkbox (unchecked by default) / Option B: leaving the community equals leaving all groups in the community / Option C: no whole-community exit is offered, groups can only be left one by one. Recommended: A. Rationale: recoverability: a user may only want to leave the community announcements while keeping frequently used groups, and the checkbox makes leaving cleanly in one step possible. Background: one product leaves the announcement group on exit (sources conflict), and one has no separate exit action. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-38] Topic lifecycle**: Option A: after topics are enabled there is one non-deletable default topic (carrying messages from before enabling, can be hidden); topics can be created (name required), closed (no more speaking), reopened, and deleted; creating and managing are separate permission items; members can switch to viewing as a regular group, which takes effect for that member only and syncs across devices / Option B: no default topic, and topics can only be created and deleted. Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: the default topic ensures old messages and uncategorized posts have somewhere to go after topics are enabled, and closing is recoverable where deleting is not. Background: only one product has in-group topic sections, and the other carries them with sub-channels. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-39] Unread and mute scope inside topics**: Option A: each topic counts unread, @me, and reaction unread independently, and can be muted and pinned separately (pin cap single digits), with the group entry showing a combined total of all topics / Option B: topic unread is merged into group unread, and only the whole group can be muted. Whether an @ inside a topic penetrates mute, see NOTI-20. Recommended: A. Rationale: user understanding: the point of a topic group is to read and mute separately; protocol specs likewise support counting unread per thread independently. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-40] Message-level reply threads**: Option A: not offered, only quote reply / Option B: any message can expand into a reply thread, the thread has its own reply count and unread, and threads do not nest further. Recommended: A. Rationale: fewest states in the first release, and quote reply already conveys context; only one product offers a thread view. | Level: Default-OK
- **[FEED-41] Restricting forwarding and saving of channels and stories**: See SAFE-31. Applies: channel admins or story publishers restricting forwarding and saving; for blocking screenshots see SAFE-08.
- **[FEED-42] Source marking after a channel post or story is forwarded**: See ACT-15. Applies: the source display and hiding after a channel post or story is forwarded into a chat.
- **[FEED-43] Whether an @ in topics, comment sections, and stories penetrates mute**: See NOTI-20. Applies: @me notifications produced by topic groups, channel comment sections, and story mentions.

## Must-ask states (specific to this module, supplementing the general state matrix)
- Audience change: after publishing, when someone is removed from the audience, blocked, removed as a contact, or added to the "hide from them" list, how are the feed posts and stories they have cached or are currently viewing handled; can their interface be told apart from "the other party has no updates"; after the author removes a contact, whether that contact's historical likes and comments are still visible to third parties (computed in real time per FEED-21, recommended visible to the author only); all visibility decisions are made on the server, and feed posts, likes, and comments the viewer has no right to see are not delivered to the client.
- Age and account state: minor or age-unconfirmed accounts publishing, being viewed by strangers, and being mentioned; the state of each entry when the account is penalized and barred from publishing (the server's decision prevails, and the local record is only an early prompt); for content moderation give a recommended default (such as publish first and review after, visible to the author only with a prompt when review fails, can be deleted), leaving only items such as penalty duration to the content safety team.
- Expiry boundary: a story expiring while being watched; expiring while offline; a story quoted in a chat having expired; the story expiring at the instant a reply is sent.
- Interaction in flight: when a comment, like, or reply is sent, the original post has been deleted, has expired, or its audience has changed; two devices liking and unliking at the same time.
- Publish failure: on a weak network some of multiple images fail to upload (recommended: keep the uploaded ones and retry the failed single image, do not void the whole post), video processing failure, switching to the background mid-publish, and what happens to unpublished content when leaving the publish page.
- Multi-device: when a publish, delete, pin, or audience change on one device takes effect on other devices; the placeholder for content types a device does not support.
- View records: how the list appears when receipts are off, for incognito viewing, and when the viewer has deleted their account or has blocked me.
- Author management: whether the author can delete others' comments under their own feed post (recommended yes, without notifying the commenter); the author's own feed post list entry (entered from "Me" and from the contact profile page, listing only posts visible to me) is a required companion to feed post requirements and does not go under "Suggested follow-ups".
- Block and report: how comments, reactions, and mentions from a blocked person under my feed posts are displayed; report entries for feed posts and comments must be provided in this release, and not doing so must go into a ruling; entries and attached content for reporting feed posts, stories, and channel posts (form see SAFE-09).
- Channel: a subscriber has blocked the admin; the signature on already-signed posts after the admin is revoked; a subscriber being offline when the channel is banned or dissolved; old links and search results after a channel goes from public to private.
- Comment section (if present): a commenter being banned; where notifications go for a reply to a commenter who has not joined the discussion group; where the comment section goes after the post is deleted.
- Community: a group being removed from the community; the same group being requested to join two communities; the community being full; a person removed from the community who is still in some sub-group; a member being offline when an announcement is published.
- Topics (if present): a member typing while a topic is closed; the default topic being hidden; a topic being deleted while it still has unread and @; the unread scope after a member switches to the regular group view.

## Anti-patterns
| Vague or wrong | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Publish a feed post, visibility can be set" | Write the tier list, the default tier, the blocking when a list is empty, and what excluded people see | Visibility scope is the privacy core, and writing it vaguely lets the default land on visible to everyone |
| Default audience written as "public" | State whether "public" means all contacts or everyone (including strangers), and the tightening for minor accounts | "Public" covers different scopes in different products |
| "A story disappears after 24 hours" | Also write where it goes on the author's side after expiry, expiry while being watched, and the expired placeholder for quotes in a chat | Expiry is the boundary with the most story branches |
| Deleting a feed post described as only a confirmation dialog | Write what happens to others' comments and likes, the placeholder in interaction messages, and failure retry | Deletion involves others' data |
| Viewer list described only as "can see who viewed" | Write whether the receipt switch affects it, how long the list is retained, and whether someone can be blocked directly from the list | View records are something the other party can perceive |
| Writing a channel as a "big group" | Write who can post, the signature, what subscribers can do, who can see the list, where comments go, and the retention period | Channels and groups have different permission and privacy models |
| A community described only as "putting several groups together" | Write announcement group permissions, how to join sub-groups, who can add groups, exit semantics, and the scope of community-level removal | Missing container-level permissions cause overreach or harassment |
| Topics described only as "groups can have topics" | Write the default topic, closing and deleting, topic-level unread and mute, and permission items | If the topic unread scope is undefined, badge counts will not match |

## Calibration sample
The feature most easily written vaguely: publishing an image-and-text feed post and choosing its visibility. Tapping "Publish" has three preconditions, publish permission, audience list, and network, so start a decision chain; publishing limits are a locking-type filter and go in the first step, and none of the three depend on a server response. Image count, image-text exclusivity, failure retry, and draft retention are written as body text per recommendation; values in the card are illustrative and actual values are set by the business, and they are listed one by one under the delivery draft's "Defaulted to recommendation"; the default visibility scope is a privacy default and must-ask, so leave a placeholder in the card.

Description:
1. After entering text or selecting images or video, the "Publish" button lights up; with only spaces or line breaks the button stays grayed out
2. On tap, check in order:
   a. 1-Publish permission check: whether the account is currently restricted from publishing feed posts
      i. Restricted: dialog prompt: "Your account cannot publish posts for now, and will be restored after {unlock time}", button "Got it", content is kept on the publish page
      i. Not restricted: go to the next check
   a. 2-Audience list check: when "Some people" or "Hide from some people" is selected, whether the list is empty
      i. List is empty: toast prompt: "Select at least 1 contact", stay on the publish page
      i. List is not empty or another tier is selected: go to the next check
   a. 3-Network check: whether there is a network now
      i. No network: the post is shown at the top of my feed post list in the "Not sent" state, visible on this device only, and on tap offers "Resend" / "Delete"
      i. Has network: upload and publish, return to the feed post list, and the new post is shown at the top
3. The publish page's "Who can see" defaults to {TBD: default visibility for feed posts, recommended: contacts only, the "Everyone" tier is not shown for accounts determined to be minors}, and can be changed to "Everyone" / "Contacts only" / "Some people" / "Hide from some people" / "Only me"
4. When continuing to select after 9 images are already selected: toast prompt: "Select up to 9 images", extras are not added; when a video is already selected, images cannot be selected, and vice versa, with toast prompt: "Images and videos cannot be published together"
5. If any image or video fails processing during upload: the whole post is not published and is handled as the no-network "Not sent" state, with the prompt "Some images failed to process, tap to resend"
6. After publishing succeeds, contacts within the audience see the post in their feed post list; excluded contacts and people in a block relationship cannot see it and receive no prompt
7. Leaving the publish page without publishing: dialog prompt: "Keep this draft?", buttons "Keep" / "Discard"; after choosing keep, the text and selected media are restored the next time the publish page is opened
Note: the default visibility scope for feed posts is TBD
